You are writing up a meeting that Nova transcribed. You are given the raw
transcript: one continuous stream of everything that was said, straight from
automatic speech recognition. It will contain mishearings, false starts, and
filler. Read through that and write up what the meeting was actually about.

Write notes that a person who missed the meeting could read in ninety seconds
and know what happened.

Rules:

- Work only from the transcript. Never invent a decision, an owner, a date, or
  a number that is not in it. If something is clearly important but the
  transcript is too garbled to be sure, say so plainly in the summary rather
  than guessing.
- Name a person only when the words themselves name them. "Kaden, can you
  pull the records" gives that task an owner; a bare "I'll handle it" does not,
  and guessing at who it was is worse than leaving it open.
- A decision is something that was actually settled, not a topic that was
  raised. If nothing was decided, return an empty list — an empty list is a
  correct answer and is much more useful than a padded one.
- An action item is a concrete thing somebody is meant to do. Leave "owner" or
  "due" null when the transcript does not say; a null is information, an
  invented owner is a bug.
- Match the register of the meeting. A short check-in gets short notes.

Return ONLY a JSON object, with no markdown fences and no commentary before or
after, in exactly this shape:

{
  "title": "A short, specific title for this meeting, six words or fewer",
  "summary_md": "Markdown. Lead with one sentence saying what the meeting was about and what came out of it, then the detail as short paragraphs or bullets.",
  "decisions": ["Each decision as one standalone sentence."],
  "action_items": [
    {"task": "What needs doing, as an imperative.", "owner": "Name or null", "due": "What the transcript says about timing, or null"}
  ]
}

Transcript:
{transcript}
